The workflow at a glance

Two input lanes meet at the same review gate. A run can use only selected, reviewed revisions and observations available at its decision time.

01 · AcquireFetch or export

Choose a source, symbols, 2018–2025 dates and enough warm-up.

02 · PreserveKeep the original

Retain document/API response, URL, query, source ID and hash outside the app when licensing permits.

03 · NormalizeMap fields

Separate occurrence, first-public, update, effective and score availability clocks.

04 · ValidateReject gaps

Check timestamps, symbol identity, units, coverage, duplicate rows and unsupported events.

05 · ReviewAttest evidence

Inspect raw revision and create a new eligible revision with a note. The parent remains unchanged.

06 · BindFreeze inputs

Select reviewed revisions plus strategy version, financial profile and daily accounting dataset.

07 · SimulateAdvance the clock

Only completed bars and information already available enter each decision. Orders fill later.

08 · InspectAudit and compare

Review decisions, fills, FX, actions, account value and period results. Keep every run.

Three different datasets: Daily raw prices, historical USD/SGD and splits/dividends support accounting. Intraday prices support timed execution. News, macro, sectors, ratings and disclosures support strategy decisions. A news source cannot replace missing prices or corporate actions.

The review gate

The UI's approval creates an immutable child revision. The reviewer must establish the following from source evidence; pressing Approve alone proves none of it.

  1. Identity: source, URL, license/access tier, symbol or stable instrument ID.
  2. Coverage: requested symbols, dates, lookback and no-event periods, including missing rows.
  3. Clocks: source publication, first availability, edits, score availability and action effective date.
  4. Original values: retain earliest recoverable text/numbers; label unknown earlier versions.
  5. Units: USD vs SGD, split ratios, dividend per-share amounts, consensus vs actual, ranges vs exact amounts.
  6. Cross-checks: sample source documents across years, DST/early closes, symbol changes and amendments.
  7. Rights: API terms, scraping rules, storage and private-use/redistribution limits.
  8. Repeatability: keep raw source externally if permitted; freeze exact normalized input and hashes in the run.

A sensible collection order

1 · Small and auditable

Use SPY plus a few named equities. Qualify daily bars/actions and one 2018 intraday month, then compare source rows to their originals. Test the chart and exact repeat.

2 · Public primary events

Try SEC 8-K filings and BLS releases. They are focused, dated and easier to audit than a broad news feed. Build separate reviewed streams and check unavailable periods.

3 · Broader context

Assess GDELT for geopolitical themes and EIA for oil. Keep them as distinct sources with documented publication and processing clocks. Expand to 2018–2025 only after a sample passes review.

4 · Harder provenance

Add sectors, individual ratings and Congress disclosures once original state transitions, report vintages, lawful use and first-public timing are established.

Every strategy version and run can be retained across the full 2018–2025 replay and compared by period. Repeated tweaking against those same years is in-sample research; its period chart is evidence about that history, not proof of future performance.
